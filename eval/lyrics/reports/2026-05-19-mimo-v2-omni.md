## What this tells us

**Coverage:** MiMo-V2-Omni dominates 3 of 5 categories on raw line-recall (reverb_heavy, instrumental_breaks, clean_pop) and matches Gemini's strength on multi_language. Its dense_vocal coverage is the weakest of the three — possibly an artifact of the song's repetition-heavy structure.

**Timing:** MiMo-V2-Omni systematically lands LATE across all categories. The lateness is consistent enough that it looks like a **fixed-offset bug** rather than random scatter — every fixture's signed median is in the +400 ms to +4030 ms range, all positive. The simplest hypothesis: V2-Omni timestamps to end-of-word or to a downbeat boundary rather than vocal onset.

**Implication for production:** A `--timing-offset-ms=-NNNN` calibration parameter applied per-fixture-category could pull this into wall-acceptable range without touching the underlying model. Reverb_heavy's 410 ms offset is already within wall tolerance; with even rough offset correction the others would follow.

## Recommendation

**Partial-promote candidate for reverb_heavy + instrumental_breaks.** MiMo-V2-Omni's text quality on these two categories is materially better than whisperx and Gemini. The timing offset is fixable with a per-category constant or a prompt revision asking for "vocal-onset timestamps".

Next experimental steps (not shipping in this run):

1. Re-run MiMo-V2-Omni with a prompt that explicitly asks for "the start time of the FIRST WORD of each line, not the end" — see if the offset collapses.
2. Add a `--timing-offset-ms` arg to the wrapper to allow per-backend timing calibration on the production routing path.
3. Test on the remaining 19 fixtures to confirm per-category strength is stable.
